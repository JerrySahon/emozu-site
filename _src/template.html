Our idea

Software first. A friend for the ones who need it.

Today our pilot runs on a Galaxy Watch: it reads the body, and the parent gets a quiet message on the phone. Many young children pull a watch off, so in parallel we find the form factor that bothers them least: off-the-shelf devices first, a light band with a soft, swappable friend if needed. No screen on the child and no alarm in the room.

What it is based on

The body changes first. That can be measured.

Four studies from 2019 to 2026 use a wearable sensor to catch an episode. Three of them predict it, 1 to 10 minutes ahead. The models look for this: heart rate goes up, heart rate variability goes down, and skin conductance bursts come more often.

The hard part: about 2 of 3 alerts were false.

A parent who gets pulled away for nothing stops listening. So we do not start with alerts. We start silent: record, label, and look back. “Yesterday at 16:40 it began eight minutes before you noticed” has no false alarms at all. Live alerts come later, and only if the numbers hold.

The gap

99 autistic children in the studies. None of them at home.

Home is where a parent needs the warning most. The research devices the models were trained on left the market: Q-Sensor in 2013, Empatica E4 in 2025. Home can work: in a 2025 Mayo Clinic trial, alerts from a regular watch cut severe tantrums in young children by about 11 minutes.

Roadmap

Where we want to be in 6 to 9 months.

Two tracks in parallel: the signal, on watches families can buy, and a band kids keep on. Then a paper with a clinical lead on 50 children. This is a plan, not traction. Investors: write to us for the one-pager.

NowPilot on a Galaxy Watch, one child

Running at home with raw sensor data and a labeling app for parents and caregivers. Worn all day. The question: can we warn ahead, at home?

Step 2Six families, 6 to 8 weeks

Six families are ready, from a parent community of 135 families. Same protocol, more children.

Step 3Protocol with a clinical lead

Ethics approval, consent, data rights. Five clinics signed for a pilot guide families through the same home protocol.

In parallelA form factor kids keep on

We test what bothers kids least: off-the-shelf devices first, toy prototypes if needed. Target: 80% of kids wear it 6+ hours a day.

50 children at home, with clinics and partner teams. A preprint on unique home data, proof of demand, and device prototypes if needed.
