=== en
Many parents of autistic children know the moment: you look away for a few seconds and the child is gone. Clinicians call it wandering or elopement. It is common, and it can be dangerous.

## How common it is

In a study of 1,218 children, **49%** had tried to run away from a safe place after age 4. Among those who went missing, 65% had a close call with traffic and 24% with drowning ([Anderson et al., Pediatrics, 2012](https://kennedykrieger.org/stories/nearly-half-children-autism-wander-or-bolt-safe-places)).

Water is the biggest risk. Most deaths after wandering are drownings, around 90% according to [Children's National Hospital](https://riseandshine.childrensnational.org/elopement-and-wandering-in-children-with-autism).

## Why children run

Wandering is often a way to communicate. The National Autism Association describes two directions: moving **toward** something the child loves, like water or trains, or moving **away** from noise, crowds, fears or demands ([summary](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/autism-and-wandering/)).

The second kind often follows overload: the same build-up that leads to a meltdown. Knowing your child's triggers helps here too. See [how to keep a trigger diary](@HOME@blog/meltdown-trigger-diary/).

## Layers that help

No single tool is enough. Families stack several:

1. **A written plan.** Autism Speaks has free forms: a family wandering emergency plan, a letter for neighbours, a letter for school ([Autism Speaks](https://www.autismspeaks.org/wandering-prevention-resources)).
2. **Doors and windows.** Alarms, extra locks, and simple visuals like a stop sign on the door ([Children's National](https://riseandshine.childrensnational.org/elopement-and-wandering-in-children-with-autism)).
3. **ID.** A bracelet or tag with your phone number.
4. **Swimming and water safety lessons.** Because water is the main danger.
5. **A tracker.** GPS devices show where a child is and can alert you when they leave a set zone. Some US programmes help pay for them: for example, Maryland's Autism Waiver covers GPS devices ([Pathfinders for Autism](https://pathfindersforautism.org/articles/safety/tracking-devices/)).
6. **Behavioural support.** A therapist can work on what your child runs toward or away from.
