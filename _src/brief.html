01 · The problem

Today a parent finds out by watching it happen.

The child often cannot say what is wrong. The parent looks for a trigger after the fact, and the next one comes without warning.

  • 47%of 409 parent posts are about hunting for triggersOur tagging, mostly r/Autism_Parenting
  • 68%of autistic children have been aggressive toward a caregiverKanne & Mazurek, 2011
  • 49%tried to run away after age 4Anderson et al., 2012

02 · The idea

Software first. A friend for the ones who need it.

  • Reads the bodyMotion, heart rate and HRV, and skin conductance (EDA) where the device allows. Today on a Galaxy Watch.
  • Tells the parent quietlyA message on the phone. No screen on the child, no alarm in the room.
  • A friend, not a sensorMany young children pull a watch off. Our band is light, with a removable, swappable friend on top, so it stays on. Render below.
  • Learns what helpsThe app shows the day and ranks what calms this child, by the body, not by guesswork.

The hard part

About 2 of 3 alerts were false in the one study that counts them. A parent pulled away for nothing stops listening. So we start silent: record, label, look back. Live alerts come only if the numbers hold.

04 · Why now

The models arrived. The devices left the market.

  1. 2013Q-Sensor stopped
  2. 2019First model, 20 children
  3. 202370 children, 4 hospitals
  4. 2025Empatica E4 retired
  5. 2025Galaxy Watch 8 adds skin conductance. A Mayo Clinic trial shows watch alerts work at home
  6. NowNo home data for autistic children yet. That is the gap.

05 · The plan

6 to 9 months, two tracks, to a study and a paper.

  1. Now · pilot on a Galaxy Watch, one childAt home, worn all day, raw signal, labels from parents and caregivers in our app. Can we warn ahead?
  2. Step 2 · six families, 6 to 8 weeksReady, from a parent community of 135 families.
  3. Step 3 · protocol with a clinical leadEthics approval, consent, data rights.
  4. Step 4 · clinics joinClinics sign up for a pilot, then families from your clinic join the home study with watches we provide. Your team guides families, so the protocol stays the same in every home.
  5. In parallel · a form factor kids keep onOff-the-shelf devices first, toy prototypes if needed. Target: 80% wear it 6+ hours a day.
  6. Goal · study and paper50 children at home, with a clinical lead and partner clinics.

This is a plan, not traction. Open questions: who pays, what young children keep on, the alert threshold.
