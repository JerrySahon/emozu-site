Our idea

A soft band. A little friend on top. Serious sensing inside.

Many children pull a medical sensor off. A friend is different. The friend sits on a band, the band reads the body, and the parent gets a quiet message on the phone. No screen on the child and no alarm in the room.

  • Skin conductance, EDA
  • Skin temperature

What it is based on

The body changes first. That can be measured.

Four studies from 2019 to 2026 use a wearable sensor to catch an episode. Three of them predict it, 1 to 10 minutes ahead. The models look for this: heart rate goes up, heart rate variability goes down, and skin conductance bursts come more often.

The hard part: about 2 of 3 alerts were false.

A parent who gets pulled away for nothing stops listening. So we do not start with alerts. We start silent: record, label, and look back. “Yesterday at 16:40 it began eight minutes before you noticed” has no false alarms at all. Live alerts come later, and only if the numbers hold.

The gap

99 children in the literature. None of them at home.

Home is where a parent needs the warning most. The research devices the models were trained on left the market: Q-Sensor in 2013, Empatica E4 in 2025.

FAQ

Short answers.

What is Emozu?

A research project. We test whether a soft band on the wrist of an autistic child can warn a parent minutes before a meltdown, at home.

Is it a medical device? Can I buy one?

No. Emozu is at the research stage. It is not a medical device, it does not diagnose or treat anything, and it is not for sale.

How can a band know a meltdown is coming?

Published studies see the body change 1 to 10 minutes before an episode: heart rate, skin conductance (EDA) and motion. A model learns that pattern and sends a quiet message to the parent.

What does the band measure?

Heart rate and HRV, skin conductance (EDA), skin temperature and 3-axis motion. There is no screen on the child and no alarm in the room.

How accurate is it?

Published models reach AUROC 0.71 to 0.87, where 0.5 is a coin flip. The one study that reports alerts found about 2 of 3 were false. So we start silent: record, label, look back. Live alerts come only if the numbers hold.

Why a little friend on top?

Many children pull a medical sensor off. A friend they like is easier to keep on. The friend also holds the battery, so a child can swap friends and keep the band on.

How can my family join?

Right now the pilot is closed: one child at home. Next come six families from a parent community of 135. Write to us through the form. The form is for adults, and please do not send medical details there.

I am a clinician, researcher, engineer or investor.

Write to us. We are looking for clinical psychiatrists for the study, hardware and signal engineers for the prototype, and investors for our pre-seed round.
